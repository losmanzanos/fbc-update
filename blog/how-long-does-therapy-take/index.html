How Long Does Therapy Take? A Denver Therapist’s Honest Answer

July 16, 2026  ·  By Becca Moravec, LPC, LMFT, AAMFT Approved Supervisor · 6 min read

It’s one of the first questions people ask in a consultation — sometimes apologetically, as if it’s rude to ask. It isn’t. You’re investing time, money, and emotional energy, and “it depends” is a frustrating answer to build a life decision around.

So here’s the honest version — the real ranges, what moves the timeline, and how you’ll know it’s working along the way.

The Short Answer

Research on how people actually progress in therapy is surprisingly consistent: many people notice meaningful relief within the first 6 to 8 sessions, and roughly half experience significant, measurable improvement somewhere in the 15 to 20 session range — about four to six months of weekly work. Focused, single-issue concerns often resolve faster. Deeper work — longstanding patterns, complex trauma, the stuff that’s been running quietly for decades — understandably takes longer, often a year or more.

Therapy shouldn’t feel endless. You deserve to know what you’re working toward, how you’ll measure progress — and what “done” looks like.

Why “It Depends” Is True — and What It Depends On

What you’re coming in for. “I want to stop having panic attacks before meetings” and “I want to understand why every relationship I’m in follows the same painful arc” are both excellent reasons to start therapy — but they’re different timelines. Symptom relief tends to come first; pattern change is a longer arc.

How often you go. Weekly sessions build momentum that every-other-week sessions have to rebuild. Especially in the first few months, consistency is the single most controllable factor in how fast therapy moves.

The approach your therapist uses. Modalities pace differently. For a single distressing event, EMDR can move remarkably quickly — sometimes a handful of processing sessions. Rewiring decades of relational patterning is slower by nature, whatever the modality.

What’s underneath. Sometimes anxiety is anxiety. Sometimes it’s the visible edge of something older. Your timeline can change as the work reveals what’s actually there — and a good therapist will tell you when it does, not just let sessions accumulate.

What Speeds Therapy Up

Clients who move fastest tend to share a few habits: they come in with goals (even fuzzy ones), they keep a weekly rhythm, they take the work into the week between sessions rather than leaving it in the room, and — most of all — they’ve found a therapist they actually click with. Fit is the strongest predictor of progress we know of, which is why we offer free consultations before you commit a dollar. Our guide to finding the right therapist in Denver covers how to assess fit without the overwhelm.

How You’ll Know It’s Working

Progress in therapy rarely announces itself. It looks like the bad week that doesn’t spiral into a bad month. The pause before the old reaction. Noticing the pattern while it’s happening instead of three days later. People around you may see it before you do. Your therapist should be checking in on progress explicitly — and if you’re ever unsure whether therapy is working, that question itself makes an excellent session topic.

Ending Is Part of the Plan

Good therapy is designed to end. From the first session, the quiet goal is to make ourselves unnecessary — to help you internalize the tools, the self-understanding, and the steadiness so you don’t need a weekly appointment to hold onto them. Many clients taper: weekly becomes biweekly becomes monthly check-ins, and the door stays open for tune-ups when life delivers something new.

The Real Answer

Come in with a focused concern and a weekly rhythm, and a few months may genuinely change things. Come in with something older and heavier, and it will take longer — and be worth it in a different way. Either way, you don’t have to commit to forever. You have to commit to starting.

If cost is part of your calculation — and for most people it is — our transparent guide to therapy costs in Denver and our walkthrough of insurance and superbills in Colorado lay out the real numbers. Or skip ahead and schedule a free 15-minute consultation — bring this exact question, and your therapist will give you an honest estimate for your situation, not a sales pitch.
