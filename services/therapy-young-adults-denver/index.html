Therapy for young adults finding their footing

Your 20s and early 30s are supposed to feel like freedom. Nobody mentioned they could also feel like one long performance review with no rubric, or that the comparison loops, career pressure and relationship questions would be this tiring.

Eighteen to thirty-five

What these years tend to ask of you

Nobody moves through these on a schedule, and plenty of people skip a stage or two. Still, these pressures come up again and again when we work with young adults in Denver and across Colorado.

  1. 18to 22

    Leaving the structure

    College, or the first year without it. Living away from home, building friendships from scratch, and finding out which of your family’s rules you actually agree with.

  2. 22to 25

    The first real job

    Rent, a boss, and a calendar without semesters. The odd sense that everyone else got a manual you somehow missed.

  3. 25to 29

    The comparison years

    Engagements and promotions on your feed, a career you’re not sure you chose, and dating with your family’s patterns still in the room.

  4. 29to 32

    The thirty checkpoint

    The quiet deadline of 30. Questions about partnership, kids, where to live, and whether the path you’re on is really yours.

  5. 32to 35

    Everything at once

    Kids or the question of kids, parents getting older, a bigger job, and less time than ever to think about any of it.

You’re building an identity while performing one online, and making choices that feel permanent before you fully know yourself. That’s a lot for any one decade.

Asking for support at 26 is a sign you’re paying attention to something that matters.

In the room

Therapy that doesn’t talk down to you

Most young adults who come to us have more self-awareness than they give themselves credit for. They know something is off, and they’ve usually tried to think their way out of it already.

We won’t assume you need to be taught how to have feelings, that everything traces back to your parents, or that you want a weekly worksheet. What you get is clinical skill and real curiosity about your actual situation: a relationship where your patterns can surface, with someone trained to help you see them more clearly than you can alone.

Sessions are 50 minutes, in person in Denver or Boulder, or online anywhere in Colorado. Our hours run until 8pm on weekdays and include weekends, which helps if you’re juggling work, school or both.

Common threads

What young adults bring us

  1. Anxiety and overwhelm

    The pressure to have the career, the relationship and the self figured out by now, when figuring it out is the actual work.

  2. Identity questions

    Who you are versus who you were told to be. Values, sexuality, ambition and spirituality, all up for examination. We are affirming of all identities.

  3. Relationship patterns

    The dynamics you keep ending up in, and what your history has to do with them.

  4. Depression and disconnection

    A flatness or lack of purpose that doesn’t match the life that’s supposed to feel exciting.

  5. Family patterns

    Understanding the home you came from, setting boundaries with parents, and keeping what’s good while leaving the rest.

  6. ADHD and feeling behind

    Missed deadlines and half-finished projects, plus the shame that piles up around them. More on ADHD therapy.
