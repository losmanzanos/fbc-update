Enneagram therapy that starts with why

The Enneagram maps nine motivations, each with its own core fear, core longing and way of protecting itself. In therapy it helps explain why you do what you do, and what it would look like to loosen your grip a little. In Denver and online across Colorado.

Two people can behave identically for completely different reasons. The Enneagram can tell them apart.

Motivation over behavior

A map of why you do things

Most personality frameworks describe behavior. The Enneagram describes the motivation underneath it.

That makes it useful in therapy, where understanding the root of a pattern matters more than labeling the pattern. Your type gives you a starting point: the fear you’re organized around, the strategy you reach for under stress, and the blind spots that come with it.

We work with type and subtype, meaning how your type shows up in self-preservation, social and one-to-one settings. We look at the automatic habits of thought and emotion each type falls into, and at what growth looks like from where you actually are, rather than from some generic model of health. Knowing your type is the starting line. The real work is using it to relate to yourself and other people differently.

The nine types

How each type tends to show up in therapy

These are tendencies we see, not rules, and most people recognize a little of themselves in several types. The names come from the Narrative Enneagram tradition.

The lines show how each type tends to shift under stress and in moments of security. The nine points fall into three centers: body, heart and head.

Body center
8

The Protector

Often tests early whether the therapist can handle directness. Once it feels safe, letting someone see their softer side is where things start to move.

9

The Mediator

Easygoing and agreeable in session, sometimes about everything. Much of the work is noticing their own preferences, and their anger, and letting both count.

1

The Perfectionist

Arrives with a clear goal and a strict inner critic. Growth often begins with noticing the frustration tucked under all those shoulds.

Heart center
2

The Giver

Talks easily about everyone else’s needs. The harder question is what they want when nobody needs anything from them.

3

The Performer

Wants to do therapy well and show progress. Slowing down enough to feel something, without turning it into a goal, is the real work.

4

The Romantic

At home with deep feeling and wary of being misunderstood. Therapy often helps them find what’s already good in an ordinary day.

Head center
5

The Observer

Tends to think things through before feeling them. Works best with room to take their time and keep some privacy.

6

The Loyal Skeptic

Watches closely at first for whether the therapist can be trusted. Later the focus often shifts to trusting their own judgment.

7

The Epicure

Brings energy, ideas and a quick reframe. The growth edge is staying with a painful feeling a little longer than is comfortable.

Who teaches it here

Two certified Enneagram teachers

Becca Moravec is a Certified Narrative Enneagram Teacher and Practitioner through The Narrative Enneagram, a lineage that emphasizes first-person accounts of each type over test results and abstract theory. She weaves the Enneagram through individual and couples work, often alongside IFS and EMDR.

Mark Whitney is a Certified Enneagram Teacher. He uses the Enneagram alongside parts work to understand what drives automatic responses, and brings in EMDR or the Safe and Sound Protocol when the roots go deeper.

Becca sees clients in person in Denver and online, and Mark sees clients online anywhere in Colorado. Individual sessions are $200 with Becca and $180 with Mark, and couples sessions are $200 with either. We’re out of network with insurance and provide a superbill after each session.

There’s no set length for Enneagram therapy. Some people come for a few months to understand their type and the pattern that brought them in. Others use it as one thread in longer-term work on anxiety, people pleasing or a relationship.

On types and testing

The Narrative Enneagram is a specific lineage within Enneagram work. It emphasizes first-person accounts from people of each type and focuses on the lived experience of the type rather than behavioral descriptions or test scores.
