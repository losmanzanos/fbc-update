LGBTQ+ affirming therapy, as you are

You shouldn’t have to educate your therapist about who you are, or wonder whether they’re quietly judging you. At Full Bloom we are affirming of all identities and orientations. You can bring your whole self here, and the focus stays on what you came in to work on.

Book a Free Consultation What to expect ↓
Who we see

The same reasons anyone comes in, and a few more

LGBTQ+ clients come to therapy for anxiety, depression, relationships, grief and work stress, like everyone else.

Sometimes identity is central to the work and sometimes it barely comes up. Both are fine. What matters is that you get to decide, and that the person across from you already understands the basics, so the hour goes to what you came for.

Every therapist at Full Bloom is affirming of all identities and orientations. We see LGBTQ+ individuals and couples in person in Denver and Boulder, and online anywhere in Colorado, with hours seven days a week.

In practice

What affirming care looks like in the room

A lot of LGBTQ+ people have a story about a provider who made an appointment harder than it needed to be. These are some of the things people describe, and what we do instead.

What people often run intoWhat you can expect here
Often elsewhere

Spending your first sessions explaining terms, pronouns or how your relationship works.

Here

We know the vocabulary. If you use a word we haven’t heard, we ask once, then get back to what you came in for.

Often elsewhere

A therapist who assumes your identity must be the source of your anxiety or depression.

Here

Your identity is never treated as the problem. We look at what’s actually causing the distress, including the strain of living in a world that isn’t always welcoming.

Often elsewhere

Feeling nudged to come out, pick a label, or tell your story before you’re ready.

Here

You set the pace on all of it. Questioning, undecided and private are all fine places to be.

Often elsewhere

Couples work built on assumptions about who plays which role.

Here

Couples therapy grounded in your actual relationship, using approaches like the Gottman Method and Emotionally Focused Therapy.

Often elsewhere

Being told to give an unaccepting family more time, or to stop being so sensitive.

Here

Room to decide what kind of relationship you want with family, set boundaries, and grieve what hasn’t been there.

What we help with

Common reasons LGBTQ+ clients reach out

  • Coming out, deciding if, when and how, and handling the reactions
  • Identity and self-understanding, exploring orientation and gender without pressure to land anywhere
  • Family stress with relatives who are struggling, distant or rejecting
  • Relationships and couples of every shape, including premarital counseling
  • Religious trauma, untangling shame from faith communities that weren’t affirming
  • Anxiety and depression, including the toll of minority stress and always being on guard

You shouldn’t have to spend your session explaining who you are. You should get to spend it on what you came for.
