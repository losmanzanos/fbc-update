For pre-licensed therapists

Where much of the real training happens

Good supervision is less about grading you and more about backing you up.

We supervise pre-licensed therapists working toward an LPC or LMFT in Colorado, from your first month of hours to your last. Supervision is where you bring the session that went sideways and get honest feedback from someone who has been there. Techniques matter. So does learning how you, specifically, show up in the room.

The goal is sharper clinical skills, a clearer sense of who you are as a therapist, and holding on to the reasons you got into this work in the first place. A brand-new candidate and someone a year from licensure need different things, and we adjust to the stage you’re actually at.

On the agenda

What a supervision session covers

Bring your real questions, your stuck cases, and how this work is actually landing on you. Most sessions touch several of these.

  • Case consultation that sits with complexity and resists the urge to oversimplify
  • Ethical guidance based on your actual cases, which are rarely as tidy as the textbook versions
  • Documentation and clinical decision-making, reviewed together so the reasoning is clear
  • Skill development through honest feedback, with plenty of encouragement too
  • Countertransference, because who you are in the room matters as much as what you know
  • Nervous system awareness, since this work is hard on clinicians and we take that seriously
  • Identity-affirming support, especially for LGBTQ+, BIPOC and neurodivergent clinicians in a field that wasn’t always built for them
  • Professional development toward licensure and the kind of practice you want to build

Supervision should feel a lot like good therapy. Honest, focused on growth, and built on trust.

First steps

How to begin

  1. Reach out

    Use our contact form or call 720-767-9808, and tell us where you are in your hours and which license you’re working toward.

  2. Talk it through

    We discuss your clinical setting, what you’re looking for in a supervisor, and whether our structure meets your board’s requirements.

  3. Write the contract

    Together we put your goals in writing, along with how we’ll work toward them and how often we’ll meet.

  4. Meet regularly

    Individual or group supervision, in Denver or online, adjusted as you grow and as your caseload changes.

We currently supervise LPCCs and MFTCs working toward full licensure in Colorado, and welcome clinicians from diverse backgrounds and theoretical orientations. If you’re still choosing a supervisor, our post on what to look for as a new therapist covers the questions worth asking anyone you interview.
