Pre-marital counseling, while things are good

Most couples spend far more time planning a wedding than preparing for a marriage. Pre-marital counseling in Denver and online across Colorado gives you a structured place to talk about money, conflict, family and the future before any of it turns into a fight.

Why before

The easiest time to do this is when nothing is wrong

Pre-marital counseling is for couples who want to walk into marriage clear-eyed, usually while things are going well.

Learning each other’s values, how you each handle conflict, and where your expectations quietly differ is a gift to the two people you’ll be in ten years. A couple of months of sessions now can spare you some very hard years later.

Our pre-marital work is informed by the Gottman Method, and Gottman’s research consistently points to how couples handle conflict, more than whether they have it, as a predictor of how relationships fare. So a good part of what we do is practice. You have a real disagreement in the room, and you learn how to come back from it while the stakes are low.

The conversations

Eight things to talk about before the wedding

These are the topics we come back to most. Some couples have already covered all of them. Most discover one or two where they have been assuming agreement without ever saying it out loud.

  • Money

    • Joint accounts, separate ones, or some of each
    • The debt each of you is bringing in
    • What counts as a big purchase, and who gets a say

  • Conflict

    • What each of you does when you’re upset
    • How an argument usually ends
    • How you make up afterward

  • Family & in-laws

    • Holidays, and whose family gets which
    • How much say your parents get
    • Family habits you want to keep, and the ones you don’t

  • Kids

    • Whether, when, and how many
    • How each of you was disciplined growing up
    • What you’d do if it doesn’t go to plan

  • Sex & intimacy

    • How often feels right to each of you
    • How you bring up what you want
    • What closeness looks like outside the bedroom

  • Roles at home

    • Who does what, and who notices what needs doing
    • Whose career takes priority, and when
    • The mental load of running a household

  • Stress & support

    • What each of you needs on a bad day
    • How you ask for help
    • How you’d handle a loss, an illness or a layoff

  • The long view

    • Where you want to live, and for how long
    • Faith, values and traditions
    • What you each picture ten years from now

You don’t need to settle every item. The point is to know where you each stand, and to have practiced talking about the hard ones before life forces the issue.

Most people spend more time planning a wedding than preparing for a marriage. A few months of sessions is a small correction.

The assessment

How we use Prepare-Enrich

We offer pre-marital counseling using the Prepare-Enrich assessment, a research-validated tool that has been used with over 4 million couples worldwide. It produces a customized profile of your relationship’s specific strengths and growth areas across more than 20 dimensions.

Each of you completes the assessment online, which takes about an hour. Your therapist uses the results to focus your sessions on the two of you, so you spend your time on what matters for your relationship instead of a generic curriculum.

  1. A free consultation

    A 15-minute call to talk through your timeline and what you’re hoping to get out of the program.

  2. The assessment

    You each take Prepare-Enrich online, on your own time and without comparing answers.

  3. Reading your results together

    Your therapist walks you through your profile: the strengths to lean on and the places your expectations differ.

  4. Sessions on what matters most

    The rest of the program focuses on your growth areas, with practice in conflict and communication built in.

Who it suits

Couples we see for pre-marital work

You don’t need to be in trouble to benefit. The couples who get the most out of this tend to come in curious rather than defensive.

  • Newly engaged couples with a year or more before the wedding
  • Couples a few weeks out who want to make good use of the time they have
  • Couples already living together who know the day-to-day but haven’t talked through the long term
  • Second marriages and blended families, where kids, exes and finances add layers
  • Intercultural and interfaith couples deciding which traditions to carry forward
  • Couples of every background and configuration, in a program that is secular and grounded in relationship science

If things already feel tense and the date is close, that’s a good reason to come in, and your therapist may suggest some couples therapy alongside the program. Many engaged couples do the whole thing online from home, anywhere in Colorado, which makes it easier to fit around work and wedding planning.
