Therapy for men, laid out plainly

Most men who come to therapy have been handling things on their own for a long time. That’s usually what they saw growing up, and asking for help can feel like it costs something. Here is what it actually involves.

Book a Free Consultation Who to see ↓
Before the first call

Most men put this off for a long time

By the time most men book a first session, something has been off for a while: sleep, temper, a marriage, or the sense that work is the only place they still feel competent.

Men come to us for many of the same reasons anyone does, plus a few that come from being raised to carry things alone. Depression in men can look less like sadness and more like irritability, numbness, working all the time, or one drink that has turned into three. Stress tends to show up in the body before anywhere else: a tight jaw, a stomach that won’t settle, waking at 4am and running numbers.

Therapy for men in Denver and online across Colorado starts with the situation in front of you. You don’t have to arrive fluent in feelings or sure of what you want. You bring what’s going on, and we work on it together, at a pace that doesn’t feel like an interrogation.

The plain version

What actually happens in a session

No couch, no mystery. If you like to know what you’re signing up for, these are the specifics.

Therapy sessionThe specifics
Length
50 minutes. Most people come weekly at first. How often you meet after that is something you and your therapist decide together.
Where
On secure video from anywhere in Colorado, or in person at our Denver office. Mark Whitney, our male therapist, works online only, so a session can happen from a home office or a parked car on your lunch break.
Hours
Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm, and weekends 9am to 5pm. Practice hours run into the evening on weekdays, which helps around a full workday.
The first session
Mostly questions. What brought you in, what’s going on at work and at home, what you’ve already tried, and what you’d like to be different. You don’t need a clear goal yet.
Who does the talking
You, more than you might expect, but it’s a two-way conversation. Your therapist asks questions, reflects what they’re seeing and points out patterns that are hard to spot from the inside.
Your childhood
Comes up only if it’s relevant and you’re ready. Plenty of men start with a practical problem, like a marriage under strain or a temper at work, and stay there for a while.
Homework
Sometimes. Something to notice, a conversation to try, a way to cool down when you feel your fuse getting short. Nothing graded.
Privacy
What you say stays between you and your therapist, with a few legal exceptions around safety that we explain at the start.
Cost
$180 a session with Mark. Our other therapists charge $150 to $200. We’re out of network and give you a superbill to submit to your insurance for possible reimbursement.
When it ends
When you’ve got what you came for. We check in regularly on whether it’s working, and you can stop at any point.
Reasons men come in

What we work on with men

Most men arrive with one of these and find out it’s connected to another.

  • Stress and pressure from work, money and family that has been building without a release valve
  • Depression that looks more like irritability, numbness or withdrawal than sadness
  • Anger, or the tight control that keeps it in and costs you closeness and ease
  • Relationship strain, including a partner asking for more than you know how to give
  • Identity and purpose at 30, 40 or 50, when performing and providing stop feeling like enough
  • Trauma from abuse, accidents, combat or loss that never got talked through
  • Fatherhood, and wanting to parent differently from the way you were raised
  • Leaving a faith you grew up in, and working out who you are outside it

“Many of my clients can already name the problem. They feel the exhaustion, the resentment, or the distance. What they can’t see yet is what keeps driving it.”
