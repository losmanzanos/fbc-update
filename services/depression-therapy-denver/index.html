Depression therapy for the heavy days

Sometimes depression is dark rooms and not getting out of bed. Just as often it’s showing up to everything and feeling nothing. Either way it’s treatable, and you don’t have to grind through it alone. We see clients in Denver and online across Colorado.

Two ways it shows up

The dark kind and the running-on-empty kind

Depression is one of the most common things people see us for, and one of the most misunderstood. The version most people picture is real. So is the quieter version, where life looks fine from the outside and feels hollow from the inside.

The dark kind

The running-on-empty kind

Mornings

The dark kindGetting out of bed takes everything you have, and some days it doesn’t happen.

The running-on-empty kindYou’re up on time with the coffee made and the inbox open. You can’t remember the last morning you looked forward to anything.

At work

The dark kindConcentrating is hard, small tasks pile up, and you may be calling in sick more than you’d like.

The running-on-empty kindYou’re still performing, maybe even well. It takes twice the effort it used to, and nobody can tell.

With people

The dark kindYou cancel plans, stop texting back, and feel like a burden when you do show up.

The running-on-empty kindYou go to everything and feel like you’re watching it from behind glass.

In your body

The dark kindHeavy limbs, sleeping too much or too little, an appetite that’s way up or way down.

The running-on-empty kindA tiredness that sleep doesn’t touch, and a body that always feels a little run down.

In your head

The dark kindA steady voice saying you’re worthless, or that nothing is ever going to change.

The running-on-empty kind“I should be grateful. Other people have it worse.” Numbness gets mistaken for being fine.

Plenty of people move between the two, or live with some of each. Depression also travels with anxiety a lot of the time, which can look like constant dread with no energy to do anything about it. If you’re wondering whether it’s depression or exhaustion, this piece on burnout may help.

Hopelessness is one of the symptoms of depression. It feels like an honest read on your life, and it usually isn’t.

Recognizing it

Signs it might be depression

Clinicians usually look for several of these together, most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. You don’t need to tick every box to come in. If something has felt off for a while, that’s reason enough to talk to someone.

  • A low, empty or flat mood most of the day
  • Losing interest in things you used to care about
  • Sleeping much more or much less than usual
  • Changes in appetite or weight
  • Fatigue that rest doesn’t fix
  • Trouble concentrating or making decisions
  • Guilt or a sense of worthlessness out of proportion to what’s happening
  • Feeling slowed down, or restless and unable to settle

If you’re having thoughts of death or suicide, please call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time. If you’re in immediate danger, call 911.

Denver, online, or both

Depression therapy in Denver and online in Colorado

Jillian Corpora and Kelli Ruhl see clients in person at our Denver office and online. Kirsten Adorno works online only, anywhere in Colorado. Jillian is warm and practical and especially easy to talk to if you’re new to therapy. Kelli brings in somatic work, parts work and skills from DBT, ACT and CBT. Kirsten looks at how relationships and old patterns feed a low mood, and she offers culturally responsive care.

Online sessions can be a real help when depression makes leaving the house feel like a lot. Some people start online and switch to the office later, or the other way around. Colorado’s dark winters can make things worse for some people, and seasonal depression is worth naming early if your mood follows the calendar.

Sessions are 50 minutes and $150. We’re out of network with insurance and provide superbills for possible reimbursement. The first step is a free 15-minute consultation, and we’ll help you figure out which therapist fits.

Asked often

What people want to know first

Depression is usually identified by a pattern of symptoms, like low mood, lost interest, sleep and appetite changes and fatigue, that lasts more than two weeks and gets in the way of feeling like yourself. If you’re unsure, that’s a good thing to talk through in a free consultation.

Not always. Therapy on its own often helps a great deal with mild to moderate depression. For more severe depression, therapy and medication together are often recommended. We don’t prescribe, but we can help you think through what to ask your doctor and work alongside them.

It depends on how long you’ve felt this way, what’s underneath it, and how it shows up. Many people feel noticeably better within 12 to 16 sessions. Others stay longer, especially when depression is tied to older patterns or past experiences.

Medication and therapy work in different ways. Therapy looks at the thoughts, habits, relationships and past experiences that feed depression. Many people who found medication wasn’t enough on its own see more improvement once they add therapy.

They overlap. Burnout is usually tied to a specific source, like work or caregiving, and tends to ease with real rest and change. Depression tends to follow you everywhere, including into the things you used to enjoy. A therapist can help you sort out which it is, or whether it’s some of both.
