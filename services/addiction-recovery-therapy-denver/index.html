Addiction and recovery therapy, wherever you’re starting

Substance use usually starts as a solution. It numbs something, quiets something, or gets you through something. Therapy helps you understand the job it has been doing and build a life that doesn’t need it anymore. In person in Denver and online across Colorado.

Starting point

No rock bottom required

You don’t need a diagnosis, a crisis, or a firm decision to quit before you start therapy. Wherever you are with it is a fine place to begin.

Some people come in already sober and want support staying that way. Some are cutting back. Some are still figuring out whether they want to change anything, and that is a reasonable thing to bring to a therapist. We work with alcohol and drug use, and with the anxiety, depression and trauma that so often travel with it.

For many people, the substance has been managing pain from things that happened to them. When that’s true, recovery means more than stopping. It means understanding what the substance was helping you survive, and finding other ways to carry it.

Where people are when they call

Every stage of recovery, including the messy ones

Recovery rarely moves in a straight line. These are some of the places people are when they first reach out, and what therapy tends to focus on there.

  1. Wondering

    You’re not sure it’s a problem. You’ve noticed you drink or use more than you planned, or you’ve started keeping count.

    Looking honestly at what it does for you and what it costs, with no pressure to label it.

  2. Deciding

    Part of you wants to change and part of you doesn’t. Both of those make sense.

    Sorting out what you actually want, and making a plan you believe in.

  3. Early recovery

    The first months, when everything feels raw and the old way of coping is gone.

    Skills for cravings, sleep, strong feelings and the hours that used to be filled.

  4. Staying steady

    Years in, and dealing with what sobriety brought to the surface.

    Trauma, grief and relationships, and a life that feels worth protecting.

  5. After a slip

    A relapse can bring a flood of shame and the urge to hide from everyone.

    Getting back on track and learning from it, without starting from zero.

People move between these, sometimes in the same month. A slip doesn’t send you back to the beginning.

The way we work

Person-centered, practical and trauma-informed

Our addiction work is led by Kelli Ruhl, a Licensed Addiction Counselor (LAC) in Colorado as well as a trauma therapist. Her foundation is person-centered: you’re the expert on your life, and you set the goals.

  1. No lectures, no labels

    We work on the goals you choose, at a pace that makes sense. Moderation, sobriety and “I don’t know yet” are all starting points.

  2. Skills from DBT, ACT and CBT

    Practical tools for cravings, urges, strong emotions and the thoughts that pull you back.

  3. Parts work

    Getting curious about the part of you that reaches for the substance, and what it’s protecting. More on that in our IFS page.

  4. Somatic work

    Calming a nervous system that has relied on substances to settle, so your body has other ways to come down.

Outpatient care

What therapy here is, and what it isn’t

Therapy with us is outpatient counseling, usually one 50-minute session a week. It works well on its own for many people, and alongside support groups like AA or SMART Recovery, medical care, or a treatment program for others. If you need detox, medication support or a higher level of care, we’ll help you think through options and can work alongside your other providers.

Kelli sees clients in person at our Denver office and online anywhere in Colorado. Online sessions can make it easier to keep showing up during a hard stretch. Sessions are $150. We’re out of network with insurance and provide superbills for possible reimbursement. If trauma, anxiety or depression is part of the picture, we can work on that at the same time.

If you’re in immediate danger, call 911. For a mental health or substance use crisis in Colorado, call or text 988, or call Colorado Crisis Services at 1-844-493-8255.
