Postpartum and perinatal therapy, without the brave face

Pregnancy and the first year with a baby can turn your whole life over. Even when everything goes well, there’s loss mixed in with the joy. We offer postpartum therapy in Denver and Boulder, and online across Colorado.

Not your fault

Common, and very treatable

Almost nobody talks honestly beforehand about how hard this season can be.

About one in five people experience a perinatal mood or anxiety disorder. These are medical conditions, not character flaws. They don’t reflect how much you love your child, how capable you are as a parent, or whether you were ready. They reflect the enormous physical and hormonal shifts of the perinatal period, on top of broken sleep and a complete change in daily life.

Perinatal covers the whole stretch: pregnancy, birth, and the first year or so after. Struggles can start at any point in it, and they often creep in gradually, sometimes months after the baby arrives, when everyone assumes you’ve settled in.

Sorting it out

What’s common, and when to reach out

Normal covers a lot of ground after a baby, and plenty of advice that makes you doubt yourself. Use this as a rough guide, not a diagnosis. If you can’t tell which column you’re in, that’s reason enough to call us.

Common

Hard, and expected

Usually eases with rest, support and time.

  • Tearful, up-and-down days in the first two weeks, often called the baby blues
  • Feeling overwhelmed and badly underslept
  • Missing your old life while loving your baby
  • Checking on the baby at night, then settling back to sleep
  • Feeling touched out by the end of the day
  • A bond with your baby that builds over weeks rather than arriving all at once
Worth a call

Time to get support

Not an emergency, and very treatable.

  • Low mood, flatness or numbness that lasts longer than two weeks
  • Worry that keeps you awake even when the baby is asleep
  • Intrusive, frightening thoughts you can’t shake, even though you would never act on them
  • Anger or rage that feels out of proportion and scares you
  • Replaying the birth, nightmares, or avoiding anything that reminds you of it
  • Feeling cut off from your baby or your partner for weeks at a time

Get help today if you notice

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room if you or your baby are in danger.

  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or that your family would be better off without you
  • Thoughts of harming your baby that feel like urges rather than unwanted fears
  • Confusion, seeing or hearing things others don’t, or going days without sleep. These can be signs of postpartum psychosis, which is rare and a medical emergency.
  • 988Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Call or text, any time.
  • 1-833-852-6262National Maternal Mental Health Hotline. Call or text, any time, in English or Spanish.
  • 1-800-944-4773Postpartum Support International HelpLine, for support and local resources. It isn’t a crisis line.
The range of it

What perinatal therapy covers

  1. Postpartum depression

    Persistent sadness, flatness or disconnection that doesn’t lift, including depression that doesn’t look like the version people expect.

  2. Postpartum anxiety

    Relentless worry, intrusive thoughts, hypervigilance about your baby’s safety, or being unable to rest even when the baby sleeps.

  3. Birth trauma

    A labor, delivery or NICU stay that left you with flashbacks, avoidance, or a body that stays on high alert.

  4. Pregnancy loss

    Miscarriage, stillbirth, termination or infertility. Losses that often get brushed aside and deserve real grief support.

  5. Anxiety and depression in pregnancy

    Struggles that begin before the baby arrives, including pregnancy after a loss.

  6. Partners and dads

    Non-birthing parents struggle too, and often quietly. We see fathers, partners and co-parents in this season as well.

In session

What postpartum therapy looks like with us

We start where you are. Some sessions are about getting through this week: sleep, asking for help, the conversation you need to have with your partner. Others go slower, into grief for the birth you expected, the identity shift of becoming a parent, or old family patterns that show up the moment you have a child of your own.

For birth trauma, EMDR can help a frightening memory lose its charge. For pregnancy loss, our grief and loss work gives that grief room. When the strain is landing on your relationship, couples therapy can help you find each other again. We also work with your OB or psychiatrist when medication is part of the picture.

Sessions are 50 minutes, in person at our Denver office, at our Boulder office with Kelsey Bennett, or online anywhere in Colorado. Many new parents choose online sessions in the early months because they fit around feeds and naps, and nobody has to get the car seat in.

Fees run $150 to $200 a session depending on your therapist. We’re out of network with insurance and provide a superbill after each session that you can submit for possible reimbursement. See our FAQ page for current rates.
