Therapy for women who carry a lot

Some experiences are particular to women’s lives: the invisible labor, the socialized self-doubt, the steady pressure to be more accommodating and more productive at the same time. Therapy is a place to stop managing all of it and start understanding it.

Book a Free Consultation Who to see ↓
Who comes to see us

Capable on the outside, tired underneath

Many of the women we work with have spent years taking care of everything and everyone, and only recently noticed what it has been costing them.

They’re usually high-functioning. They run teams, households, group chats and family logistics, and from the outside it all looks handled. Inside there’s anxiety, a short fuse, a sense of being disconnected from their own life, or a flatness they can’t explain.

Therapy for women in Denver at Full Bloom is individual therapy with a feminist, intersectional lens. We take seriously the context women live in, so a normal reaction to a hard situation doesn’t get treated as a personal flaw. We’re also direct. If we see a pattern, we’ll say so, kindly.

The roles

The jobs that come without a title

Much of what women bring into therapy comes from roles like these. None of them are bad on their own. The trouble is that they tend to run all at once, mostly unnoticed, and the person holding them rarely gets a turn to set them down.

  1. The reliable one

    The first call when something goes wrong, and the last person anyone thinks to check on.

    In therapy

    Noticing the automatic yes, and practicing a no that doesn’t come with three paragraphs of apology.

  2. The household project manager

    Holds the calendar, the grocery list, the pediatrician’s number and the camp sign-up deadline in her head, all at once.

    In therapy

    Naming the mental load out loud, then working out what can be handed off and how to ask for it.

  3. The family translator

    Still managing her parents’ feelings, sometimes from two states away, and smoothing over every holiday before it starts.

    In therapy

    Boundaries with family that you can hold without guilt running the conversation.

  4. The one who never drops a ball

    Perfectionism that looks like high standards from the outside and feels like dread from the inside.

    In therapy

    Where the standard came from, what it protects you from, and what good enough might look like.

  5. The body under review

    Food rules, the voice in the mirror, and the pressure to bounce back after a baby or a hard year.

    In therapy

    Weight-neutral work on food and body with therapists trained in Intuitive Eating.

  6. The woman in transition

    Divorce, a new baby, a career change, an empty nest, or turning 40 and wondering whose life this is.

    In therapy

    Making sense of who you are now, without rushing to become the next version.

How the work goes

No script and no set curriculum

We start with what’s happening in your life right now, the patterns that keep showing up, and what you want to change. Some weeks we trace a pattern back to where it started. Other weeks we get practical about the conversation you’re dreading this weekend. Underneath, we draw on whichever of these fits.
